# Open Protocol implementation plan - 2026-10-21

A plan for [`spec.md`](spec.md) (2026-10-21), whose fifteen open questions were ruled as recommended the same day and whose review's ten findings ([`open-protocol-spec-review.md`](../../../reviews/open-protocol-spec-review.md)) were applied the same day. It has the shape of the PLCs plan ([`../plcs/plan.md`](../plcs/plan.md)) and the flows plan ([`../flows/plan.md`](../flows/plan.md)): prerequisites that are each useful alone, then the spec's pieces in dependency order, each phase a PR with its own tests, and three milestones that merge end to end. Its review ([`open-protocol-plan-review.md`](../../../reviews/open-protocol-plan-review.md), fifteen findings) was folded the same day, each as recommended, and the issues are filed under the label `open-protocol`: [#461] is the parent, each phase below names its issue, whose body is the plan's text at filing and is refreshed from here after a fold, and the milestones are [I](https://github.com/Jde-cpp/opc-hub/milestone/23), [II](https://github.com/Jde-cpp/opc-hub/milestone/24) and [III](https://github.com/Jde-cpp/opc-hub/milestone/25).

Line numbers cite opc-hub2 at `af6ea641`, main with the web and OpcServer refactors merged, as the spec does since the fold; open62541 refs are v1.5.9. Refs re-anchored on 2026-10-23, the UA server library's move among them, cite branch `servers-1a-ua-server-library`. Section and table numbers are the Open Protocol specification's, Release 2.20.1, as in the spec.

Five things the spec's piece table does not say:

- **Piece 3 is two phases, and it goes first.** The codec with no I/O, a header and field codec and a type per MID tested from byte buffers, is reviewable before a socket exists and is where the fuzz target lives; the session and the controller side come after and test against each other. Nothing in either touches the gateway, so both start now.
- **The row follows the session.** The spec has pieces 1 and 3 independent, and the columns, the save rules and the loopback dial are; `testConnection` is not, since on an Open Protocol row it speaks MID 0001, 0002 and 0003 and reports the identity. So the row lands after the session, with its Test Connection whole, rather than before it with the identity deferred.
- **The host lands with the driver's skeleton.** Piece 2 proves nothing with no driver behind it, and a stand-in driver would be thrown away. Phase 4 carries the host and the first slice of the Open Protocol driver, the row's strand, the session and `_Op`, so the host's tests run against a real row and a real controller link; `Result` and `Counters` are Phase 5.
- **The server library lands first for Phase 4 on.** Every phase from 4 builds on `Jde.Opc.UAServer`, the servers plan's phase 1 ([#380], [#381]), and the hub's web sessions on the in-process lookup of its phase 2 ([#382]). Phases 1 to 3 and the emulator's station touch none of it.
- **Conformance is two checks, not one.** Jarrekstar's simulator runs on a developer's box as soon as the commands exist and covers the session, the state, the results and the commands; the bench controller the spec's *Open questions* #15 names, a PF6000 with two virtual stations, covers what the simulator lacks, backfill, link level, alarms, the clock and the Disconnect action. The first is a check anyone can run; the second is a procurement.

## Phase 1 - the codec (piece 3, first half)

Issue [#463].

`Jde.Opc.OpenProtocol` in `libs/opc/openProtocol/`, beside `libs/opc/shared` as the servers plan's `libs/opc/server` is, added to the root [`CMakeLists.txt:47`](../../CMakeLists.txt#L47) after `Jde.Opc`; it links `Jde` and nothing of open62541, and its headers go under `include/jde/opc/openProtocol/`. The header: twenty ASCII bytes, the length, the MID, the revision, the no-ack flag, the station, the spindle, the sequence number and the two part fields, then the data field and a NUL the length does not count; the receiver finds the end by the length, since MID 0900 carries binary after its NUL ([§2.2, §2.3][op]). The decoder bounds the length at 9999 ([§3.8][op]); reads each part field as one character by §7.1's table, `1` to `9`, `A` to `Z` and on to 99, a space not used, and refuses one outside it ([Table 1, §7.1][op]; plan review #10); refuses a field that is not its width, a digit field with mixed content and a missing NUL; and reads a digit field that is all spaces as its default, the table's where it names one, revision 1, station 1, spindle 1, and not-used otherwise ([§2.2.2, Table 1][op]; review #1), and a revision of `000` as 1, which the tables name revision 0-1 (plan review #10). The encoder writes digits. A type per MID the driver speaks, 0001 to 0005, 0014 to 0018, 0034 to 0038, 0042, 0043, 0050 to 0054, 0060 to 0065, 0070 to 0077, 0080, 0081 and 9997 to 9999, each with its revisions' fields: MID 0002's identity through revision 6 ([Tables 13–20][op]), MID 0004's code with its name ([Table 23][op]), MID 0061 at revisions 1 to 7 ([Tables 108–114][op]) and MID 0065, a layout of its own and a subset of 0061's, with no limits, no parameter set name and no time of its last change, at 1 to 7 ([Tables 123–129][op]; plan review #5), their torques as the wire's integer over 100 and their time as `YYYY-MM-DD:HH:MM:SS` with no zone, which the codec hands up unconverted, MID 0052 at 1 and 2, MID 0071 and 0076 at 1 to 3, MID 0015 at 1 to 3, MID 0035 at 1 to 6, MID 0018 at 1 and MID 0038 at 1 and 2 ([Tables 38–40, 69–75][op]; plan review #11). A message decodes by the revision in its own header, whatever was asked (review #4), and encodes at the revision the caller names. Log is an `OpenProtocol` value of `ELogTags` ([`logTags.h:8`](../../include/jde/fwk/log/logTags.h#L8)).

The specification's copy is the one OpenProtocolInterpreter's repository carries ([PDF][op]), since Atlas Copco publishes none, and the PF6000 appendix is on ServAid ([PF6000 appendix][pf6kapp]); neither goes in the repo. The codec's test vectors are transcribed from their tables, each case naming its table and MID, so a reviewer with the PDF checks a case in one look.

Tests: `Jde.Opc.OpenProtocol.Tests` in `libs/opc/openProtocol/tests/`, on the pattern of `Jde.Opc.Lib.Tests` ([`libs/opc/shared/tests/CMakeLists.txt:6`](../../libs/opc/shared/tests/CMakeLists.txt#L6)), registered with `addJdeTest` and added to the root's test list after the opc library's ([`CMakeLists.txt:73`](../../CMakeLists.txt#L73)): a round trip of each MID at each revision; the specification's example frames decoded, the MID 0001 of §5.2.1 with its nine blank header fields and §1.4's MID 0052 answered at revision 1 to an ask at 2 ([§1.4, §5.2.1][op]); the refusals, a length past 9999, a part character outside §7.1's table, a short field, a mixed digit field, a missing NUL; a header whose revision is `000` read as 1; a MID 0061 at each of revisions 1 to 7 from the tables' rows, and a MID 0065 at each of 1 to 7 from its own, the one at 7 with its station id. The fuzz target: `Jde.Opc.OpenProtocol.Fuzz`, a libFuzzer executable over the decoder behind a `JDE_FUZZ` option that is off by default, built with clang's `-fsanitize=fuzzer,address` on Linux and run by hand for an hour before each milestone closes; its corpus, the suite's frames plus what it finds, is checked in under `libs/opc/openProtocol/tests/corpus/`, and the suite replays the corpus through the decoder as an ordinary case, so a crash the fuzzer found stays fixed on both runners and the CI, which runs plain `ctest` ([`linux-ci.yml:81`](../../.github/workflows/linux-ci.yml#L81)), never needs the fuzzer. Built 2026-11-12, where it differs from the text above:

- **Nothing throws.** `Decode`, `DecodeHeader`, `DecodeBody`, `Encode` and `FrameSize` ([`Codec.h`](../../include/jde/opc/openProtocol/Codec.h)) answer a `std::expected` whose error is a `Refusal`, a code and a sentence that names the MID, the revision, the parameter and its byte as the tables count it. The header decodes apart from the data field, so a session can acknowledge at link level a frame whose data field it then refuses, and the codes tell a MID with no type from a revision past a type's last, the controller side's MID 0004 codes 99 and 97. The encoder refuses what does not fit its field, a parameter set of 1000 or a VIN of 26 characters, where the plan has Phase 8 check before it sends.
- **A type is the union of its revisions' members** ([`Mids.h`](../../include/jde/opc/openProtocol/Mids.h)), `Mid0061` and the rest under the names the spec's *Address space* gives `Result`, a MID with no data field a `NoData`; one layout per type is walked by a reader and a writer alike ([`Layouts.h`](../../libs/opc/openProtocol/src/Layouts.h)). A number is an `optional`, empty where its field is all spaces, Table 3's value not supported or not defined, and written back as spaces; a text comes without the spaces after it; a time is a `local_seconds`; a torque is a `double`, written to the nearest hundredth.
- **A number in the data field may have spaces after it.** The specification's own MID 0061 and MID 0065 examples write their tightening ids left-justified, `345675` and four spaces, against Table 3's zeroes on the left ([§5.8.2, §5.8.6][op]), so the decoder takes both. Spaces before or between the digits are refused, and the header's fields keep Table 1's rule, all digits or all spaces. The spec's *The codec* says so.
- **Refused beyond the plan's list:** a data field shorter than its revision's table; a parameter id that is not its row's; a number past its range, a tightening id past 4294967295 or a job sequence number past 65535; a control byte in a text, below 0x20; a time not of digits in the shape `YYYY-MM-DD:HH:MM:SS`; part fields that disagree; and the data field of one part of a linked message, whose header still decodes.
- **A text byte past ASCII is Latin-1's character.** Table 3 gives a text 0x20 to 0x7F, but a refused name would lose the result it came with, so a byte from 0x80 to 0xFF is read as the Latin-1 character and held as UTF-8, as an OPC UA String, the web's JSON and a protobuf `string` need. The encoder writes U+0020 to U+00FF back as their bytes and refuses the rest, a width counting characters. Latin-1 maps each byte to one character, so whatever code page a controller writes, every byte goes back as it came; Phase 10b sees which the PF6000 writes (code review #1). The spec's *The codec* says so.
- **A time that is no date is a time not set.** Digits in a time's shape that name no date or time of day, `0000-00-00:00:00:00`, day 00 or `24:00:00` among them, read as unset, as a field of spaces does, so a controller's placeholder costs the field and not the result it came with (code review #2). A time not of that shape is still refused, as a frame that has lost its place. The codec does not log it, so the driver cannot tell the two unset times apart; the bytes are in Phase 2's frames at Trace. The spec's *The codec* says so.
- **Bytes past a revision's last field are ignored and logged.** A controller whose firmware appends a parameter to a MID keeps its results: the codec reads the revision's fields and logs what follows under its tag, at Warning the first time the process sees it for a MID and revision and at Debug after (code review #3). The cost is a later revision's data field under an earlier revision's header, read by the earlier layout from bytes that are not its fields where the two differ: MID 0004 revision 2's three-digit code under a header of revision 1 reads as code 00, its last digit logged. The fuzz target calls `Logging::Init` with no settings, so no line is kept. The spec's *The codec* says so.
- **The log tag is the library's own**, `OpenProtocol::LogTag`, named `openProtocol`, at bit 48 ([`logTags.h`](../../include/jde/opc/openProtocol/logTags.h)), registered through `Logging::AddTags` by each main that links the library, as `Opc::TagNames` is, and not a value of the framework's enum. Since [#556](https://github.com/Jde-cpp/opc-hub/pull/556) the framework's own bits end at 31, and a line under a library's tag does not trap the debugger at the break level ([`logTags.h:86`](../../include/jde/fwk/log/logTags.h#L86)), which a controller's refused frame should not. The codec logs only the bytes it ignores past a layout; the frames at Trace are Phase 2's.
- **The revisions the text did not name:** MID 0001 at 1 to 6, with no data; MID 0064 at 1 to 7, the revision of MID 0065 it asks for, as MIDs 0014, 0034, 0051, 0060 and 0070 carry the revision of what they subscribe to; MID 0004 at 1 and 2; MID 0074 at 1 and 2; every other MID at 1.
- **The specification's misprints** are named where the code and the cases meet them: Table 39's Byte column from the angle max on, Table 107's for parts 3 and 4, Table 159's 60-110 for the alarm text's 50 characters, and Table 23's second 102. §5.8.6's MID 0065 example is a byte short of its own length and of Table 123, and is left out of the examples the suite decodes.
- **The suite's frames are written from the tables' Byte columns** ([`Cases.cpp`](../../libs/opc/openProtocol/tests/Cases.cpp)): a row gives the byte it starts at, checked against the bytes written before it, so a width that drifts from its table fails the case, and each frame is decoded and encoded against. `TableTests.CasesCoverEveryRevision` requires a case for each MID at each revision, 100 of them.
- **The fuzz target** is under `tests/fuzz/` ([`CMakeLists.txt`](../../libs/opc/openProtocol/tests/fuzz/CMakeLists.txt), which heads with its run lines). It compiles the codec's sources itself, so they carry libFuzzer's coverage instrumentation, and links libstdc++ by file name beside the build's libc++, which the packaged runtime needs. It and `CorpusTests` run one harness ([`Harness.h`](../../libs/opc/openProtocol/tests/Harness.h)): nothing crashes, and a frame the decoder takes encodes to one that decodes to the same message. Since Phase 2 it also streams each input through the session's framer (Phase 2's *Built*). The corpus starts as 118 seeds, the suite's frames, each required on disk as the suite has it, and the suite also runs every seed with each byte changed in turn and cut short at each byte, which is what a runner has of the fuzzer. The target ran thirty minutes at the build, 138 million inputs with no finding, and the 325 of its inputs that reach code the seeds do not are merged into the corpus. After the code review's fixes it ran ten minutes twice, 45 and 43 million inputs with no finding, and the 122 of their inputs that reach code the corpus did not are merged too, 565 files; the hour is each milestone's.

## Phase 2 - the session and the test controller (piece 3, second half)

Issue [#464].

`Session`, in the library: an asio TCP socket on an executor the caller hands it, the gateway's `Executor()` ([`execution.h:10`](../../include/jde/fwk/process/execution.h#L10)) wrapped in a strand per row, and a `Settings` struct, `keepAlive`, `replyTimeout`, `reconnectMin`, `reconnectMax`, the per-MID revision ceilings and `log`, which the driver fills from the block in Phase 4 and the suite fills directly. On connect it sends MID 0001 from revision 6, steps down on MID 0004 codes 74, 76 and 97 under the ceilings ([§5.2.1, Table 23][op]), reads MID 0002 by its own header's revision and keeps that as the settled one for the connection's life (spec *The protocol*; review #4), and hands up the identity. One request outstanding at a time per direction, a queue behind it ([§3.2.1][op]); the step-down per MID applied to each later request and each subscription, 0060 from 7, the alarms from 3, 0051 from 2, 0014 from 3, 0034 from 6 and 0038 from 2 (plan review #11), so a push is decoded by its header and the settled revision is known per MID. Link-level acknowledgement, MID 9997 and 9998 against a sequence number running 1 to 99 per direction, where MID 0002 revision 6 says the controller numbers its messages, and the per-topic acks, 0062 for a result, 0016, 0036, 0053, 0072, 0077, otherwise (spec *Open questions* #7); a repeat of the number last acknowledged is a retransmission, re-acknowledged with the same 9997 and dropped, and any other number out of order is answered by 9998 carrying the expected one, then a disconnect and a reconnect ([§3.2–3.2.2][op]; review #5). MID 9999 after `keepAlive` without a message either way ([§5.28.1][op]); a request or an ack unanswered within `replyTimeout` is a lost link ([§3.3.5][op]); reconnect with the backoff doubling from `reconnectMin` to `reconnectMax`; MID 0003 on a clean close. A push reaches the caller's callback on the strand with its ack already sent, so the driver's write never delays the ack (spec *Results*). `Connected` and `Lost`, with the socket's or MID 0004's error, are callbacks too. Every frame in and out at Trace under the tag when `log` is on.

`Controller`, in the library for the suites and the emulator (spec *Open questions* #12): an acceptor on a port, a session per client, scripted per case: the revision it answers per MID, 0004 code 97 above it; its MID 0002 identity, with revision 6's station id and whether it numbers its messages; a result store with tightening ids global and non-consecutive across two stations, which answers MID 0064 by id and 0 as the latest ([§5.8.5, Table 121][op]) and serves `Push( result )` to a subscribed client; the selected set, job and VIN, and an alarm, pushed on subscribe when scripted to and on change; a clock for MID 0080 that the script may skew; whether a 0001 revision 8 asks for the tool lock, refused; and the faults, drop the link, delay one reply or several, go silent, send a malformed frame, drop a client silent past its timeout, 15 s on a controller and scaled down in a test ([PF6000 manual][pf6k]). It refuses a second Actor with code 35 and a Viewer's command with 92 ([§3.3.4][op]) when a case asks for them.

Tests, in the library's suite on an ephemeral port, the session against the controller: the step-down from 6 to a controller answering 97 above 3, and the MID 0002 at 3 to a MID 0001 at 6 read by its header; 0061 pushes at 3 to a 0060 at 7; link level taken when revision 6 says so and application acks when not; a repeated sequence number re-acknowledged and dropped, any other out of order answered 9998 and reconnected; the keep-alive holding a controller that drops at a scaled silence; a reply past `replyTimeout` a lost link, the backoff doubling between attempts; a malformed frame refused without a disconnect of its own. The timing cases scale `keepAlive`, `replyTimeout` and the backoff to hundreds of milliseconds, so the suite stays far inside the 300 s ctest timeout ([`functions.cmake:180`](../../build/functions.cmake#L180)). Size M.

Built 2026-11-12, where it differs from the text above:

- **Two handles over private implementations**, [`Session.h`](../../include/jde/opc/openProtocol/Session.h) and [`Controller.h`](../../include/jde/opc/openProtocol/Controller.h), with what the two sides share, the sequence numbers and the socket, in [`Wire.h`](../../libs/opc/openProtocol/src/Wire.h), and the frames by their lengths in [`Framer.h`](../../libs/opc/openProtocol/src/Framer.h). A session's `Start`, `Request` and `Stop` are called from any thread and post to the strand, and every callback runs on it. `Stop( stopped )` answers what is queued with `Stopped`, and where the link is up sends MID 0003 behind the request out, which keeps its answer, since the controller takes one request at a time ([§3.2.1][op]), then closes. A request out with no answer within `replyTimeout` of the stop is answered `Stopped`, and the socket closes with no MID 0003 (code review #2); once `stopped` has run nothing is called and nothing of the caller's is held. A session destroyed without it closes with no MID 0003.
- **A request answers through a callback**, `Request( body, onReply )`, with the message that answered it, MID 0005 or its data, or a `Failure`: a kind, the MID, MID 0004's or MID 9998's code and a sentence. The kinds are what Phase 8 maps: `Refused` for MID 0004, `Timeout`, `NotConnected` for a request made while the link is down or still queued when it went, `Stopped`, and `Value` for a request the codec does not encode, with nothing sent. `Lost` hears a `Failure` too, `Socket` and `Sequence` among its kinds, for the link's loss and for each attempt that fails, so `_Op.LastError` follows every attempt.
- **Revisions are kept by the MID asked.** `Revision( 60 )` is what MID 0060 was accepted at, then the revision in the header of the last MID 0061 pushed, and `Revisions()` the settled ones, MID 0001's among them; Phase 4 writes `_Op.Revisions` from it under the MIDs the spec names. A ceiling named for the MID a request asks for bounds the request, so the row's `{"0061": 3}` caps MID 0060, and one for MID 0071 or 0076 caps MID 0070. MID 0074's revisions are its own and settle nothing. A request queued behind another of its MID starts at the revision that one settled.
- **The attempt has the reply's deadline**: the address resolved and the socket connected within `replyTimeout`, a host name through asio's resolver and each address it gives. ## Risks

- **No hardware before 10b.** The suite's controller shares the driver's codec, so it proves the two sides agree, not that either agrees with a controller; the specification's tables are the codec's oracle, and 10a's simulator is a second implementation for the session, the state, the results and the commands. The walk's station filter, link level, the alarms and the clock are the controller's alone.
- **The specification's copy.** Release 2.20.1 is a third party's copy of a document Atlas Copco does not publish; a controller's firmware may follow a later release, and the other vendors keep their own MID tables. The step-down, the row's ceilings and decoding by the reply's header are the defence, and 10b's captures replace the tables where they differ.
- **Writes under the service lock.** A fill of `maxBackfill` results at some forty fields each is tens of thousands of `UA_Server_writeDataValue` calls, posted from the strand to the server's thread, where each takes the lock while the collector's subscription samples and every client's request waits behind them. Phase 6's fill case is the check; the host logs how long the writes held the lock at shutdown, as `UAHistory::Reads` does, and a fill that shows there is grouped per result.
- **Threading on Windows.** `UA_Server_*` from the row's strand relies on `UA_MULTITHREADING` at 100, which the superbuild does not pin ([`build/CMakeLists.txt:491`](../../build/CMakeLists.txt#L491)) and the servers plan flags as unverified on Windows ([`../servers/plan.md`](../servers/plan.md) *Risks*); Phase 4 inherits that check.
- **The rights lookup under the service lock.** Phase 8's `INodeAccess` runs in the method callback under the service lock and must stay a lookup, `Authorize::Rights` with the criteria overload, never `Gate`, the rule the servers spec's *Threading* states.
- **The suite's clock.** Keep-alive, reply timeout, backoff and the 15 s controller drop are seconds on a line and must be hundreds of milliseconds in the suite, so every timing key is a `Settings` field the fixture scales; `Jde.Opc.Tests` runs in about 14 s today against a 300 s timeout ([`functions.cmake:180`](../../build/functions.cmake#L180)), and the fill case lowers `maxBackfill` in the suite's block.
- **The controller's slot and its Disconnect action.** The gateway's connection is one more beside the line PLC's, and a controller with one slot gives it up; a virtual station whose Disconnect action locks the tool stops the line whenever the gateway restarts. Both are the site's to settle before the row is added, and the help says so from 10b's own observation.
- **The time zone.** The controller's clock is local to the plant and the gateway's default zone is the server's, UTC on many; a row left at the default on a server in UTC stamps every result hours off. The form shows the zone and the help names the trap.
- **Ports.** `portBase + id` collides with a service on the box only by accident; the start-up error names the port and the key.
- **The servers plan's pace.** Phases 4 to 10 wait on [#380] and [#381], the hub's web sessions on [#382] and Phase 8 on [#383] or its own map. Phases 1 to 3, 11 and the form half of 12 land without them.
- **Spec drift.** A PR that changes behaviour changes `spec.md` with it; 10b's findings are the fold this plan still asks for, the queue limit and the plan's other decisions being folded with its review (plan review #14).
